These are study-defined annual overheating exposure bands.
The reviewed sources provide different temperature metrics, assessment periods, and population assumptions.
They do not validate 50-hour and 100-hour thresholds as moderate and high health-risk classes for this metric.
Green represents shorter annual exceedance duration; a health assessment requires additional evidence.

## Standards and literature

**CIBSE TM59 (2017).** The bedroom criterion permits operative temperature above 26 °C for at most 1% of annual night-time hours.
The assessment period is 22:00–07:00, and 33 or more exceedance hours constitute failure.
A Building Simulation 2023 study explicitly applies these criteria. [IBPSA paper, methodology](https://publications.ibpsa.org/proceedings/bs/2023/papers/bs2023_1217.pdf).
Those criteria concern bedroom night-time comfort at 26 °C.
Applying their hour limit to annual whole-building temperatures above 30 °C would change the metric.

**CIBSE TM59 (2026).** CIBSE's interview with the lead author describes a revised bedroom criterion using mean night-time temperature.
It uses a 27 °C threshold and allows at most four exceedance nights per summer.
This criterion replaces the previous hourly bedroom criterion. [CIBSE Journal, August 2026](https://www.cibsejournal.com/technical/tm59-stress-testing-homes-for-overheating/).
CIBSE recommends the 2026 edition as current practice.
Its publication page states that England's Part O compliance still uses the 2017 edition. [CIBSE publication page](https://cibse.org/knowledge-research/knowledge-portal/tm59-overheating-risk-in-dwellings-a-design-stage-methodology-2026).
Neither criterion establishes the annual exposure bands used in this figure.

**Health Canada (2026).** Guidance for older adults recommends an upper indoor temperature limit of 26 °C.
It identifies sustained exposure between 26 °C and 31 °C as a concern, especially overnight and across consecutive days.
It identifies temperatures above 31 °C as a threshold for substantially greater health impacts.
The guidance records evidence limitations and population differences. [Health Canada, sections 2–5](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/publications/healthy-living/guidance-upper-indoor-temperature-limits-protecting-older-adults.html).
This guidance supports assessing indoor heat exposure and its duration.
It provides temperature guidance for a specified population, rather than annual operative-temperature hour classes.

## Figure interpretation

Use the term **annual overheating hours** in the figure and caption.
Describe the colours as **annual overheating exposure bands**.
The annual counts retain exposure duration but omit the sequence of exceedance hours and occupant vulnerability.
Separate assessment of night-time conditions and consecutive exceedance periods would support further interpretation of heat hazard.
